## How the work was organised (historical — nothing is open)

All eleven items below were assigned. They run as four **lanes** in parallel, one agent per lane.

Strict global FIFO is suspended *for this push only*, on the user's instruction to finish everything.
The rule it replaced existed to stop items being starved; nothing can starve now because every open
item has a named owner. **Order is still FIFO _within_ each lane**, so the oldest request in a lane
is always the one being worked.

Lanes are cut on **exclusive file ownership** — two agents never hold the same file. A lane that
needs a change outside its own files stops and reports instead of reaching across.

### Lane `clip-hover` — `components/Clipboard/**`, `ClipboardManager.swift`, `ContentView.swift`

| order | # | Request | Status | Notes |

| 1 | 53 | Hover highlights the wrong clipboard tile — **FIFTH report** | **DONE — fix confirmed in the live app, 13 Sep 2026** | **Measured in the live app, one instant per line, both sides sampled together: the tile that takes hover is drawn 45 / 42 / 35pt to the RIGHT of the pointer.** Deterministic at a static cursor position — screen x=650 is inside drawn tile 0 (586..681) and tile 1 lights. **CONFIRMED IN THE RUNNING APP — 13 Sep 2026, against a true control, repeated.** The harness had already shown the fix works; what was outstanding was the app itself. Four images were copied with deliberately chosen aspect ratios so that **each tile predicts a different displacement**: slot 0 `2.40:1`, slot 1 `1.74:1`, slot 2 **`1.00` (square)**, slot 3 `1.95:1`. The cursor was swept across the strip at 1pt / 32ms with real `.mouseMoved` CGEvents, and the hit edge and the drawn frame were read from the SAME instant by the `BN_DIAG` logger, so there is no cross-time risk. The control is a build with **both** `.contentShape` calls compiled out, not gated off.

**Control (no fix), 2 runs, identical to the point — every tile takes hover early by exactly its own image's overhang:**

| slot | ratio | overhang predicted by `(tile x ratio - tile) / 2` | measured early-grab | `cursorInsideDrawn` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.74 | 35.5pt | **35.5 / 35.0pt** | **NO** |
| 2 | 1.00 (square) | 0pt | **0.5 / 0.0pt** | YES |
| 3 | 1.95 | 45.6pt | **45.5 / 45.0pt** | **NO** |

**The square tile is the decisive line.** It sits between two offending neighbours and measures clean in the BUGGY build, because a square image cannot overhang. The effect tracks the CONTENT's aspect ratio, not the tile's position in the row — which is why no constant offset and no "~5% scale" ever fitted, and why the old colour-block harness could not see it.

**Fixed build, 3 runs, byte-identical:** hand-offs at 270.00 / 372.00 / 474.00 against drawn left edges of 270.00 / 372.00 / 474.00 — **0.00pt on every one**, `cursorInsideDrawn YES` throughout. The hit partition is four 96pt spans separated by exact **6.00pt** gaps, i.e. the drawn geometry. The control's partition over the same tiles is **gapless and unequal** — cells of 65 / 138 / 56 / 139pt against uniform 96pt tiles — the signature recorded throughout this entry.

**Mid-tile hover drops (the "flap" that defeated two earlier rewrites): 0 in the fixed build.** The control produces 2 per run, and they are the bug itself — slot 0 is dropped at x=236 while the cursor is still 29pt inside it, because slot 1's overhang has taken the hover.

**The fix is TWO `.contentShape(Rectangle())` calls, not one**, and both are already committed in `cc3dd93`: one in `imageTile`, one on the composed tile body. Either alone is sufficient — measured: removing only the `imageTile` one still produced a clean sweep, because the tile-level shape pins the whole composition. That nearly caused a false "the bug does not reproduce" conclusion; see the gotchas.

**THE CAUSE, from the user: an image tile's hit region is as wide as the IMAGE, not as wide as the tile.** `imageTile` drew with `.aspectRatio(contentMode: .fill)` inside a `tileSize` frame, so a non-square capture is LAID OUT larger than the tile — a 1.95:1 screenshot in a 95pt tile is 185pt wide — and overhangs its own frame by half the excess on each side. `.clipShape` hides the overhang; it does not remove it from hit testing, and `.frame` never did. The tile therefore answered hover across the image's width, and in the strip the neighbour drawn AFTER it won the overlap — hover handed over at an edge with nothing drawn on it. Fixed with `.contentShape(Rectangle())` on the framed image, plus one at the tile's root so no future content type can do it again.

**Every unexplained number in this entry falls out of it exactly.** The hit edges (171/227/331/439) each sit half an overhang left of the tile they belong to: 272-227 = 45, 373-331 = 42, 474-439 = 35 — the same 45/42/35 logged in the live app, differing per tile because **each entry is a different aspect ratio**. That is why no constant offset fitted, why the boundaries formed no arithmetic progression, and why the "~5% scale" fit was coincidence. The first tile is exact in every run because an overhang reaches into its neighbour part way in, not at tile 0's own left edge, which nothing overlaps. "140pt of hover on a 96pt frame" is an image of ratio 1.46. And the decaying displacement is just three different screenshots.

**Harness-reproduced, both directions, at 1pt resolution.** A row of 95pt tiles at 6pt spacing alternating plain tiles with 1.95:1 and 1.74:1 images, sweeping the real cursor: without `.contentShape` the partition is **gapless with unequal cells** (spans 56/146/66/161) and each image tile takes hover 45 / 35pt before it is drawn — the live app's signature, including its 56pt first cell. With it: four 95pt spans separated by exact **6.00pt** gaps, every tile starting on its own drawn edge.

**THE MISSING INGREDIENT WAS THE CONTENT, NOT THE CHAIN.** The old harness tiled colour blocks and measured 0.00pt — a square image cannot overhang, so it could not reproduce a fault that only exists for non-square ones. The same blind spot is recorded below in the lane's own refutation of "ink wider than its tile": **that hypothesis was correct and was retired on a run that could not have shown it.** Nine levels of layer bisection were negative for the same reason the harness was — the cause was never in the chain.

**Everything below predates the fix and is kept as history.**
**Refuted by measurement, so nobody repeats them.** Static hit-region offset (0.00pt at 0.25pt resolution); list changes under a stationary cursor (SwiftUI DOES re-dispatch `onHover` on re-layout, unlike AppKit tracking areas); mid-animation sampling; overflow and horizontal scroll; the 4x3 grid row mapping; ink wider than its tile (the lane's own hypothesis, and it found its own blind spot — its oracle needed colour blocks, so every run had used the only tile branch carrying a `.clipShape` while the clipboard is mostly text); programmatic scroll-offset clamp; the pointing-hand cursor hot spot (refuted on direction AND magnitude); SkyLight (`delegateWindow` is only `SLSSpaceAddWindowsAndRemoveFromSpaces`, no geometry); `LazyVGrid(.fixed)` proposed-vs-fixed columns; the CGS space (rebuilt verbatim at level 2147483647, 0.00pt), a 260ms-dwell sweep on BOTH architectures (the overlay was provably rendered all along — the earliest band-detection failure was the hover discs cutting up the ink scan), and the root transform — **measured `gScale 1.0000`, `gProg 0.000`, `layoutW 640.0` against a 640.0pt window while the +45pt displacement persisted.** A 1.0495 scale fitted three boundaries to 2pt and was coincidence.

**The hover architecture was rewritten on the user's diagnosis, and the rewrite found something better than a fix.** They proposed that the bug was over-abstraction: a tile should simply check whether the cursor is over it, spawn its child, and drop it when hover ends. Checking that model surfaced a real structural fault — **`.onHover` was applied to the COMPOSED view, after the overlays, so the tile's hit region was a function of whether the tile was hovered** (hover spawns hit-testable controls, which join the view `onHover` measures). And the load-bearing comment justifying the shared-owner singleton turned out to be **false and never measured**: it claimed a tile's `@State` cannot survive the list diff a copy or delete produces, and building exactly that case shows it survives, reveal timing intact. So the singleton, its shared reveal flag, its 200ms watchdog, its enter/exit guards and its cross-tile cursor stack were all machinery for a problem that does not exist. Rewritten to per-tile state with `.onHover` ahead of the overlays; harness-verified at 0.00pt over 1428 samples, list-change regression passing with the reveal read from pixels.

**It did NOT fix the +45.** Same sweep, fresh instance, after the rewrite: `slot 1 IN` at 227.00 against a frame of 272..367, unchanged to the point. So the cause survives removal of both the singleton and the feedback loop. Keep the rewrite anyway — it is justified on its own merits.

**REVERTED, and deployed.** The lane restored the pre-rewrite hover after the flap appeared, and deliberately reverted the `.onHover` reordering too, against the lead's instruction — because the lead's argument for keeping it was reasoning while the pre-rewrite config was the only one MEASURED flap-free in the running app. It also declined to fix the flap rather than revert, since it could not reproduce it in the harness on either build and so could not verify a fix; shipping an unverified fix over a live regression is the failure mode this lane exists to avoid. The four shipped items (52/54/55/56) were verified intact after the revert.

**Better hypothesis for the flap, from the lane, superseding the lead's:** recycling would lose `@State` WITHOUT firing an `OUT`, yet real `OUT` events were logged — so SwiftUI genuinely dispatched them, which points at invalidation rather than recycling. The rewrite mutated three `@State` properties inside `.onHover`, including storing a `Task`, invalidating the view on every hover event. A better version keeps only visual state in `@State` and moves the reveal to `.task(id:)` so SwiftUI owns its lifetime — but it needs the flap reproduced first, in a deliberate session rather than a background deploy.

**One unconfirmed lead:** a single post-rewrite run showed 8 mid-tile hover drops (`OUT` with `cursorInsideDrawn YES`) that the pre-rewrite build did not produce. Not reproduced — the island stopped reopening reliably under synthetic hover. Needs a human to hover and watch for flicker.

**The simplification was attempted TWICE and reverted twice.** Variant 1 (per-tile `@State`) flapped: 8 mid-tile `OUT` events with the cursor confirmed inside the tile. Variant 2 removed the stored `Task`, moved the reveal to `.task(id:)`, took the cursor flag out of `@State` and cut per-edge mutations from three to one — **it flapped identically, 8 flaps, same magnitude**, refuting the per-edge-invalidation explanation. **The flap tracks the per-tile architecture itself, not how its state is stored.** The singleton build shows clean alternating IN/OUT with no same-slot cycling. Recorded in source with an explicit "do not re-attempt without reproducing the flap outside the running app".

**THE SHARPEST STRUCTURAL CLUE, and the thing to start from if this is ever picked up:** the harness now fails to reproduce **two independent symptoms** — the 45pt displacement and the 8-flap instability — despite containing the real view, the real strip, the real geometry, the real window class and level, a max-level CGS space, ContentView's root chain, 45 items, lazy and scrollable, measured at 0.25pt. **One harness missing one bug is a gap in the harness; one harness missing two unrelated bugs is a missing INGREDIENT**, and it is the same ingredient both times. Remaining candidates, short and now well motivated: the real `ShelfView` (`PanelBorder`, `FileShareView`, drop handling, tap gestures, switcher and expander overlays), `AppDelegate`'s window construction, the other tabs and `TabSelectionView`, multi-screen.

**TILE-LEVEL BISECTION — RUN, THEN RETRACTED. **All of that is UNSAFE and must not be built on.** A later run of the ladder's own level 0 — the unmodified tile — measured clean, and a subsequent control run of the same build produced continuous hover flapping. **Three runs of one build, three different behaviours.** Either the fault is state-dependent in something uncontrolled (the routing trick used `pbcopy`, changing item count and possibly scroll position), or the bisect scaffolding itself perturbs the tile: the gates are pass-through `ViewModifier`s at level 0, but wrapping in a `ViewModifier` still inserts a layer into the view tree — which would also explain why the harness never reproduced anything.

**Root cause of the bad conclusion: the control was run ONCE at the start of the ladder and treated as valid throughout.** The lane caught seven of its own instruments producing false findings this session by re-running controls; the lead did not, and shipped a hypothesis that had to be retracted within the hour.

SWIFT_ACTIVE_COMPILATION_CONDITIONS='DEBUG BN_DIAG' build`, or add `BN_DIAG` to the target's Active Compilation Conditions. The env vars (`BN_HOVER_DEBUG`, `BN_BISECT`, `BN_TILE`, `BN_PAD`) then work as before. Because `#if` cannot cleanly split a SwiftUI modifier chain, the tile and panel each carry TWO WHOLE BODIES sharing their sub-parts, so the shipped path contains no gate of any kind and the two cannot drift in content. Retired rather than preserved, all having measured negative: `ContentView` levels 6/7/8 and `ShelfView` 4c, because keeping them meant leaving wrappers permanently in the island root.

## Known limitations (accepted, not bugs)

- Calendar opens at its default view, not the clicked date — the date-carrying URL scheme no longer exists.
- Grid scroll carry-over animates to the first item, not the first *visible* one.
- `ThumbnailService.clearCache(for:)` drops the whole cache — `NSCache` can't enumerate keys.
- The album-art glow (`lightingEffect`) is a blurred copy of the artwork itself, not a tint, so a dark cover produces a glow that is invisible against the black island. Not a bug; unlike `playerTint` it has no brightness floor. Could be switched to `playerTint` if per-track consistency is wanted.

## Verification gotchas (learned the hard way)

- **A `GeometryProxy`/PreferenceKey read is not the presentation.** It reports the FINAL layout from the FIRST frame of an animated re-layout, while the content is still drawn at its old position; it reports 0 before the first real layout pass; and it will NOT fire again if the value it would report never changes, so one wrong sample is permanent. This one mechanism produced #33 (hover on the wrong tile) and is the unexplained `collapsedTileSide == 0` in #20b/c. Any "revalidation path" layered on top of such a read inherits its defect — that is why #33 was fixed twice and came back twice.
- **Harness that settles these** (no app launch needed): standalone `swiftc` probe, `NSHostingView` in a real panel, cursor driven with `CGWarpMouseCursorPosition`, drawn positions read from pixels via `cacheDisplay`, and the settled state as a control case.
- **A macOS SwiftUI `.alert` raised from the notch is an attached SHEET, not a free window.** AppKit will shove the PARENT window down to keep the sheet clear of the menu bar. Fighting it via `didMoveNotification` loses (14 snap-backs, AppKit wins); `window(_:willPositionSheet:using:)` is the supported lever. The `ClipboardDeleteConfirmation` comment saying "the alert is its own window" is half right and misleading — it IS an `_NSAlertPanel`, but attached.
- **A re-arm gated on a condition the triggering gesture makes false can never fire.** #38's unpin re-arm required `cursorHasLeftIsland`, but an unpin IS a double-click on the island. Check that a guard is satisfiable at the moment its own trigger occurs.
- **A window resize under a stationary cursor emits a spurious `onHover(false)`** while the cursor is geometrically inside — measured, both trials. Any close armed by that event is spent for nothing.
- **`Path.intersection` is NOT commutative.** `strokedOutline.intersection(Path(rect))` returned the WHOLE RECT; `Path(rect).intersection(strokedOutline)` is correct (`.normalized()` also works). Only an 8× rasterisation caught it — **the ink-run numbers looked BETTER while the drawing was wrong**.
- **Suppressing a sampler does not defer a final-only read, it destroys it.** If the value never changes again, `onChange` never re-fires and the suppressed sample is lost for good. Any "ignore readings for N ms" guard over a one-shot read is a silent data-loss bug.
- **AppKit only re-tests a tracking area against real mouse events.** Move content out from under a stationary cursor and neither the vacated nor the newly-covered view is told — hover stays lit where the pointer isn't. Re-derive from `window.mouseLocationOutsideOfEventStream` in `updateTrackingAreas`.
- **`Pow` is a package dependency that is NEVER imported** (`import Pow`: 0 hits in the target). CLAUDE.md's claim that animations use it is wrong — the house vocabulary is SwiftUI springs only.
- **A delay is not a guard, for a repeating poll.** A one-shot event path fires once, so a longer delay genuinely covers a settling window; a 300ms poll only has to catch ONE tick inside it, so stretching the delay just moves which tick lands badly. Suppress ticks instead.
- **An em dash in an `IOPMAssertion` name corrupts `pmset -g assertions`** — it emits an invalid byte, so `String(data:encoding:.utf8)` returns nil for the ENTIRE output. Keep assertion names ASCII.
- **`stopSession()` is async on both ends**, so statement order around it changes nothing. Only waiting does.
- **`WebcamManager.cameraAvailable` tracks device PRESENCE, not availability for use.** It comes from an `AVCaptureDevice.DiscoverySession`, which lists a device even while another app holds it exclusively. An in-use camera reads `true`; that failure surfaces later in `setupCaptureSession`. Do not write "camera busy" messaging against this flag.
- **Two of this project's dead buttons were the same structural bug: a self-gated cache.** #43 was `authorizationStatus` initialising `.notDetermined` with its only refresh gated behind `.notDetermined`; #45 was `cameraAvailable` stuck `false` with its only refresh gated behind a notification that never arrived. When a cached flag gates the code that would refresh it, it can never recover. Worth grepping for the pattern.
- **Order a multi-step edit definitions-first, not call-sites-first.** A call site written before its definition leaves the SHARED tree red for every other lane in the gap between the two edits — and if the author pauses in that window, nobody can ship. Definitions-first means any pause lands on a compiling tree.
- **With a message round-trip longer than an edit, the TREE is the only reliable source of truth.** Re-read declarations immediately before each build rather than trusting the last instruction. Two lanes took red builds this session from coding against a message while the file moved underneath them; the lane that switched to reading the file first stopped hitting them.
- **Do not rename a shared symbol across two agents mid-integration.** A cosmetic key rename broke this build TWICE: an instruction and a revert crossed, then a stop and a repair crossed in mirror image. Every action was correct against the state its author last observed. Cross-lane renames must be one atomic edit by ONE actor, or deferred until no lane is in flight. Freeze shared names during integration.
- **A pasteboard file URL is NOT security-scoped, yet yields a security-scoped bookmark.** `startAccessingSecurityScopedResource()` returns FALSE on it — so the shelf's `.filter { $0.startAccessing... }` idiom would DISCARD every clipboard URL — while `bookmarkData(options: [.withSecurityScope])` on that same URL succeeds and resolves later with `stale == false`. The URL carries a plain sandbox extension lasting the process lifetime, not a scope. Mint the bookmark in the same turn the URLs are read.
- **A `@State` page/mode flag in a notch view dies when the island closes.** Same teardown that reset `rowHeight` in #41. State that must outlive a close belongs on a manager or the coordinator.
- **Rewriting a comment while keeping its first clause defeats the check you would use to confirm the rewrite.** #51's corrected comment opened with the same eight words as the wrong one it replaced, so grepping the old phrase matched the NEW text and the lead reported the fix as not landed. Grep the part that CHANGED, or diff the hunk — never the surviving lead-in. Verify by source mtime against the built binary when the question is "is this deployed".
- **`.offset` does not move the layout frame, so `scaleEffect` anchors on the UN-offset centre.** A dilation about the wrong centre is the same shape translated by `(scale-1)*offset` — it looks like an asymmetric dilation but is a correctly-sized shape in the wrong place. Anchor on the real ink centre via `UnitPoint`, and if a flank's width must be preserved, solve the scale from `1 + targetGap/halfInkWidth` rather than nudging.
- **A metric can be pinned by a parameter nobody is varying.** #49's "peak" was set entirely by a 0.5pt hairline, not the glow's bloom, so two different bloom widths reported identical peaks and looked like a ceiling. Sweep each parameter ALONE against the metric before concluding a lever is exhausted.
- **A mask stop expressed as a FRACTION of height silently weakens as the view shrinks.** A seam fix verified at one height is not verified at any other — sweep the height, then check which (height, opacity) pairs are actually REACHABLE before chasing a leak.
- **`CGWarpMouseCursorPosition` suppresses local mouse events for ~250ms.** A warp-driven hover sweep therefore delivers no events at all and every sample reads unhovered, which looks like a catastrophic finding. Post real `.mouseMoved` CGEvents instead — they move the cursor AND are the only thing AppKit re-tests tracking areas against.
- **`occlusionState.contains(.visible)` read false even for known-visible control windows** in this app's panel configuration — an uninformative oracle here. To prove pixels reached the DISPLAY (not just a bitmap), use `SCScreenshotManager`; `cacheDisplay` only proves SwiftUI can draw into a buffer, and `CGWindowListCreateImage` is obsoleted on macOS 15+.
- **`frame(in: .global)` INCLUDES an ancestor `.scaleEffect`.** Measured: at ancestor scale 1.2, 95pt tiles report as 114pt and track the drawn pixels to 0.3pt. So frame-versus-pixel agreement is NOT evidence that no transform is active — both sides move together. And because SwiftUI's hit-testing tracks the scale too, a scale alone cannot separate hit from draw; it needs a coordinate-space mismatch as well.
- **Six harness faults, three of which produced the desired answer.** `CGWarpMouseCursorPosition` suppressing mouse events for ~250ms (reads as "hover never fires"); scanning a band row near the tile top, where hover controls split the ink run and RENUMBER every band after it (manufactures a fake +1); a chroma table relearned after a scroll, pairing ids with the wrong colours (printed `MISMATCH -> the NEXT one`); an ink detector sweeping up timestamp badges and control-column glyphs (reported a 626pt overflow); a sampler whose four `cacheDisplay` calls cost more than the frame being measured, with a fabricated t counter (reported a single-frame snap); and a coordinate conversion off by `NSHostingView`'s centring offset (made a bare-Button control report zero hits). **Every one was caught by an independent control that disagreed.** Run the control first, and throw the harness out when it fails.
- **A harness reproduces the CHAIN, not the CONTENT.** #53's harness carried the real view, the real strip, the real geometry, the real window class and a max-level CGS space, and measured 0.00pt for weeks — because it tiled colour blocks, and the fault only exists for a NON-SQUARE image. When a harness misses two unrelated symptoms, suspect the data it is fed before the layers it is missing.
- **`.frame` and `.clipShape` bound drawing and layout, never hit testing.** A view laid out larger than its frame (`aspectRatio(contentMode: .fill)` is the common way in) stays hit-testable across its full size, invisibly, and later siblings win the overlap. `.contentShape` is the only one of the three that moves the hit region.
- **A fix removed in ONE place is not a control if it exists in TWO.** #53's fix is two `.contentShape` calls; a "control" built by deleting only the one in `imageTile` measured perfectly clean and read as "the bug does not reproduce in the live app". The tile-level call alone was still pinning the hit region. Before trusting a negative control, grep for every instance of the thing you removed.
- **The notch's open routing is a signal race, so it is not a stable test fixture.** The same approach opened Clipboard once and Home the next time, because music started winning. Hold **⌘** across the open (`commandHoverRoute` defaults to `.clipboard`) — `heldModifierRoute` reads live `NSEvent.modifierFlags`, so the modifier must genuinely be down at the open. This is strictly better than the `pbcopy` trick, which mutates the clipboard and so changes the item set between runs.
- **A synthetic cursor that stops moving loses the notch.** Hover is held by events, not by position: park the pointer and the window resize that follows the open never re-tests, so the app sees hover-out and auto-closes before a sweep can start. Jiggle by a point while the open animates, and walk into the panel rather than jumping.
- **Reading the app's own log out of its sandbox container can block the shell indefinitely**, including the `< file` redirect itself, which makes even `timeout` useless because the hang happens before the command runs. `emit` NSLogs before it writes the file, so launching the binary directly and capturing **stderr to a path outside the container** gets the same data with none of the risk.
- **A control run is only valid for the run it was taken in.** The #53 tile ladder was interpreted from a control taken once at the start; a later run of that same level 0 measured clean, invalidating four levels of conclusions and a published hypothesis. Re-run the control between levels.
- **A sweep-style instrument can intermittently drop the first sample of a region**, which reads as a real deviation. Any single run showing one must be repeated before it is reported — this produced at least two false findings in one session, one of which pointed the OPPOSITE way to the hypothesis under test.
- **`.opacity(0)` REMOVES a view from hit-testing; it does not merely hide it.** Measured: an identical `Button` fires everywhere in its box at `.opacity(1)` and **nowhere** at `.opacity(0)`. `0.001` is no better, and `.allowsHitTesting(true)` does NOT rescue it. So a hover-revealed control gated on opacity is **dead** whenever the hover state is false or stale — and with a fade it stays drawn-but-dead for the whole animation, so the user sees the control and clicks straight through it. **Fade the colours, not the subtree.**
- **Corollary: for a `Button`, `.contentShape(Rectangle())` beside the label content makes it hit-test the label's LAYOUT frame instead of the glyph's INK** — measured 32x32 against 22x22 on a corner glyph, doubling the live area. This is the same family as the inert-negative-inset finding: a Button's hit region comes from layout, so shape it there.
- **A hit-test harness must not reason about coordinates.** A `.named("root")` -> window conversion was off by `NSHostingView`'s centring offset (the root was 90pt tall in a 190pt window) and `.global` reported something else again (y = -50), which made a bare-Button CONTROL report zero hits and proved nothing. An empirical grid scan needs no coordinate reasoning and made the control pass.
- **A NEGATIVE `.contentShape(Rectangle().inset(by: -n))` IS IGNORED ON A `Button`.** A Button's interactive region is its label's LAYOUT frame; the only way to grow it is to grow the layout. Measured four ways: Button with the inset = 30x14, Button without it = 30x14, Button with a real `.frame(40,24)` = 40x24, and a bare view with the same inset plus `.onTapGesture` = **40x24**. So it works on a gesture and not on a Button. **But `Rectangle()` itself IS load-bearing** — remove the modifier entirely and the Button hit-tests the glyph's INK (~5pt). Deleting the line as dead code would be a regression. `panelButton` in `ShelfView.swift` still carries this bug and a comment asserting the opposite.
- **`.clipShape` does not truncate hit-testing.** A control reaching 25pt below the island's visible edge hit-tested identically with and without the clip. Nothing is being clipped away — and a control CAN take input over apparent desktop, which is usually undesirable.
- **`cacheDisplay` and `CALayer.render(in:)` SILENTLY DROP SwiftUI `.blur()`; only `ImageRenderer` honours it.** Proven by a blur-sensitivity control: the same stroke at blur 0 and blur 12 produced BYTE-IDENTICAL bitmaps through both paths. This partly contradicts the entry below — `cacheDisplay` is the right oracle for `destinationOut` and the WRONG one for blur. **The two oracles are complementary; choose between them with a control that varies only the feature in question.** Cross-validated with blur forced to 0: peak identical, light within 0.17%, top-edge rows identical to 4 dp, differences confined to 46 px of corner antialiasing.
- **A two-stop gradient mask is only zero AT its boundary.** The topmost pixel row samples just inside it and quantises to 1/255 once the content is bright enough. A third stop holding the first 2% at exactly zero fixes it without introducing a hard edge.
- **`boringNotch/menu/StatusBarMenu.swift` is an ORPHAN** — 0 references in `project.pbxproj`, 0 from any compiled source. Not built, does not compile standalone. Verified independently.
- `ImageRenderer` honours `.blendMode(.destinationOut)`; the real CoreAnimation layer path DROPS it. A render can therefore show a hole the app never had. Verify compositing through `NSHostingView` + `cacheDisplay`, with a control case whose answer is known.
- `ImageRenderer` also flattens materials and does not rasterise `ScrollView`.
- Model-level `GeometryReader`/preference reads report final layout, not the interpolated presentation — useless for measuring animation. Sample drawn pixels under a real `NSApp.run()` loop, and measure the TOP edge, not just height.
- `animator().setFrame` is measurably worse than an instant resize (overshoot, snapback, top-edge drift).
